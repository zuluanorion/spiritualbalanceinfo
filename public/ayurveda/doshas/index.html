THE FIVE ELEMENTS

Made of the same matter as the stars

For Ayurveda, the science of life, a star, a river and your own body are woven from the same five elements.

Space gives room to everything; air moves; fire transforms; water joins; earth gives form. In the body, the five come together as three living forces, the doshas. From space and air comes vata, the energy of movement. From fire and water, pitta, the energy of transformation. From water and earth, kapha, the energy of structure. In harmony, they sustain your life; but dosha also means “that which can be disturbed”: they are the first to move when life loses its order.

Everyone has all three; what makes each of us unique is the proportion. From conception, every being receives its own measure of vata, pitta and kapha, an imprint for the whole of life: this is your prakriti, your original nature. Food, climate and emotions can draw you away from that measure, and the state you are in today is called vikriti.

The key is simple: like increases like, and the opposite restores balance. Being in balance does not mean resembling an ideal or having the three doshas in equal parts: it means returning to the measure you came into life with. In the end, it is remembering who you are.

VATA · SPACE AND AIR

Vata, the wind that moves life

Everything that moves in you is moved by vata: the breath, the heartbeat and every thought that crosses your mind.

It is air moving through space. Think of the wind: dry, light, cold, rough, subtle and always in motion. Those are the qualities of vata, its gunas, and that is why you recognize them in yourself when it grows: the skin that dries out, the cold hands, the mind that will not stop. Its main home is the large intestine, and it also lives in the pelvis, the bones, the ears and the skin. It is what moves your breathing, your circulation, your voice, your senses, your nerves and elimination, and what sets the other two doshas in motion. In the mind it gives you creativity and enthusiasm; in the emotions, joy and lightness. In its subtlest form it is related to prana, the life force that enters with you in every breath.

Its five forms

Tradition distinguishes five forms of vata, according to where it acts. There is no need to memorize them; I name them so that you can see that even the smallest of your movements has its place:

  • Prana, in the head: inhalation, the senses and the mind.
  • Udana, in the chest: the voice, effort and memory.
  • Samana, beside the digestive fire: it separates what nourishes from what is left over.
  • Vyana, from the heart: circulation and all movement.
  • Apana, in the pelvis: everything that moves down and out.

In balance and out of balance

In balance it gives you lightness, fresh ideas, peaceful sleep and regular digestion. When it is disturbed, your body dries out and grows cold: dry skin, cold hands and feet, gas, irregular elimination, stiffness, broken sleep. The mind scatters and forgets. In the heart grow fear, worry and the feeling of having no ground under your feet. If you recognize yourself here, do not be afraid: it is vata asking you for warmth and rhythm.

What increases it

Everything that resembles it: too much of the bitter, pungent (spicy) and astringent tastes; cold, dry or raw food; skipping meals; dry cold and wind; late autumn and early winter; the afternoon and the hours before dawn; old age, which is its stage of life. And also the life many of us lead today: staying up late, traveling a lot, living without a schedule, too much stimulation, and fear, loss and loneliness.

How it returns to its center

With its opposite: warmth, moisture, softness and, above all, rhythm. The sweet, sour and salty tastes; warm, cooked, oily food, such as soups, stews and rice, with ghee (clarified butter) and warming spices such as ginger and cinnamon. Eating and sleeping at the same times. Abhyanga, massage with warm oil, is like an embrace for vata, especially with sesame oil. Slow movement, long breaths, meditation. And as an attitude: slowing down, trusting and letting yourself be cared for.

PITTA · FIRE AND WATER

Pitta, the fire that transforms

Everything that is transformed in you is transformed by pitta: food into life, light into sight, experience into understanding.

It is fire that lives in water: hot, sharp, light, slightly oily, liquid and flowing. Those are its qualities, and you recognize them in yourself when it grows: the heat that rises, the acidity, the impatience. Its main home is around the navel, in the stomach and the small intestine, and it also lives in the blood, the sweat, the eyes and the skin. It governs your digestion, your metabolism, the heat of your body, hunger, thirst and sight. In it burns agni, your digestive fire, the root of health; when agni weakens, what is not digested becomes ama, a heavy, sticky residue that clogs the channels. In the mind, pitta gives you intelligence and discernment; in the emotions, courage and warmth. In its subtlest form it is related to tejas, the inner light that shines in your eyes.

Its five forms

  • Pachaka, between the stomach and the intestine: it digests food.
  • Ranjaka, in the liver and spleen: it gives color to the blood.
  • Sadhaka, in the heart: intelligence and purpose.
  • Alochaka, in the eyes: sight.
  • Bhrajaka, in the skin: its glow and its color.

In balance and out of balance

In balance it gives you a good appetite, luminous skin, a sharp mind and a warmth that draws people in. When it is in excess, there is too much heat: burning sensations, acidity, loose stools, strong thirst, heavy sweating, reddened eyes, skin that is easily irritated. The mind becomes critical, perfectionist and impatient. In the heart burn irritation, frustration, anger and jealousy. If you recognize yourself here, it is pitta asking you for coolness and rest.

What increases it

What brings more fire: too much of the pungent, sour and salty tastes; fried food, alcohol, fermented foods, too much coffee and red meat; going hungry; strong sun, summer and early autumn; midday and midnight; youth and adulthood, which are its stage of life. And also competition, overwork and anger held inside.

How it returns to its center

With coolness, gentleness and surrender. The sweet, bitter and astringent tastes: sweet fruits, cucumber, leafy greens, coconut, rice, ghee, cilantro, fennel and mint. Not skipping meals, and making lunch the main meal. Cool water, never iced. Coconut oil for massage. Walking by the water or under the moon, swimming, meditation of the heart. And as an attitude: letting go of control, forgiving, laughing and remembering that life is not a race.

KAPHA · WATER AND EARTH

Kapha, the earth that sustains

Everything in you that is firm and serene is held by kapha: the strength of your muscles, the smoothness of your joints, the patience of your heart.

It is the water that the earth gathers and keeps: heavy, cold, oily, slow, smooth, sticky and stable. Those are its qualities, and you recognize them in yourself when it grows: the heaviness on waking, the slowness, the attachment to what is familiar. Its main home is the chest, and it also lives in the throat, the head, the stomach, the joints and the fluids of the body. It gives you form, cohesion and lubrication, and sustains your strength, your fertility and your natural defenses. In the mind it gives you calm, constancy and memory; in the emotions, love, patience, compassion and loyalty. Tradition says that healthy kapha is strength, and relates it to ojas, the finest essence of the body, which sustains your defenses and the joy of being alive. Nourishing it is the heart of Rasayana, the art of renewal.

Its five forms

  • Kledaka, in the stomach: it moistens food.
  • Avalambaka, in the chest: it supports the heart and lungs.
  • Bodhaka, on the tongue: the perception of taste.
  • Tarpaka, in the head: it nourishes the senses and the mind.
  • Shleshaka, in the joints: it lubricates them.

In balance and out of balance

In balance it gives you strength, stamina, deep sleep, firm defenses and a love that endures. When it builds up, everything becomes heavy and slow: sluggish digestion, little appetite in the morning, mucus, fluid retention, weight gain, too much sleep and a heavy waking. The mind clouds over. In the heart appear attachment, melancholy, a lack of drive and resistance to change. If you recognize yourself here, it is kapha asking you for warmth, lightness and movement.

What increases it

What brings more weight: too much of the sweet, sour and salty tastes; cold dairy, fried food, desserts and flour-based foods; eating too much or without hunger; damp cold; late winter and spring; early morning and early evening; childhood, which is its stage of life. And also a sedentary life, sleeping during the day, staying in comfort and holding on to what is asking to be released.

How it returns to its center

With warmth, lightness, dryness and movement. The pungent, bitter and astringent tastes: vegetables, leafy greens, legumes, light grains such as barley or millet, and spices such as ginger, pepper and turmeric. Eating only when hungry, and having an early, light dinner. Getting up before six. A vigorous massage, with very little oil or none at all. Exercise that makes you sweat. And as an attitude: letting go of what has completed its cycle, opening to the new, giving and moving.

DUAL NATURES

When two doshas join hands

Many people have two doshas in almost equal measure. If that is you, your path is to care for both, and to listen for the one that asks for attention in each season.

WIND AND FIRE

Vata-pitta

Quickness, creativity and intensity, somewhere between restlessness and irritation. The sweet taste, regularity and a warmth that does not burn care for both.

FIRE AND EARTH

Pitta-kapha

Strength, constancy and powerful digestion. The bitter and astringent tastes and movement bring them into balance.

WIND AND EARTH

Vata-kapha

Sensitivity and sweetness. They share the cold: their great medicine is warmth, in food, in movement and in affection.

ALL THREE IN HARMONY

Sama prakriti

It is rare: the three doshas in similar proportions. Tradition calls it sama prakriti, the balanced nature, and considers it the most stable.

THE SIX TASTES

What taste tells your body

Every time you eat, taste speaks to your body. In Sanskrit, rasa means taste, and also sap and essence. Each taste is born from two elements and speaks directly to the doshas: it calms some and stirs others. Tradition advises bringing all six together in every meal, with the emphasis on the ones your nature needs, and eating warm food, calmly and at regular times: how you eat matters as much as what you eat. There is no need to calculate it; with time, your body tells you.

THE FOUR PATHS

How Ayurveda restores balance

Ayurveda does not fight the body: it reminds it of its order. It has four paths, and in the consultation I decide which ones you need and when.

FOOD

Ahara

Food suited to your nature, to the season and to the strength of your digestive fire. Every meal brings you closer to your center or further from it.

THE WAY YOU LIVE

Vihara

Your schedule, your rest, movement, breathing and what you see and hear: daily life, where balance is sustained.

PLANTS

Aushadha

Medicinal plants and the preparations of the tradition, chosen for your nature and your moment, which accompany the body as it recovers its order.

THE THERAPIES

Shamana and shodhana

Some soothe, such as massage with oils or shirodhara, a stream of warm oil over the forehead. Others purify from the root: Panchakarma. Tradition teaches that what is only soothed can return; that is why, when the moment is right, it invites us to go deeper.

DINACHARYA · THE DOSHA CLOCK

Every hour has its dosha

The doshas also govern the hours, and you have surely felt it already: the heaviness if you get up late, the strong hunger at midday, the mind wide awake in the small hours. Twice a day, in waves of about four hours, kapha, pitta and vata pass through. Dinacharya, Ayurveda's daily routine, is the art of living with that wave and not against it.

6 TO 10 · MORNING AND EVENING

Kapha

In the morning it brings stability, and heaviness if you get up late. In the evening it brings calm and sleepiness: the time to go to bed.

10 TO 2 · MIDDAY AND MIDNIGHT

Pitta

At midday the digestive fire is at its peak: the time for the main meal. At midnight it digests and transforms within, including what you have lived.

2 TO 6 · AFTERNOON AND BEFORE DAWN

Vata

In the afternoon it brings creativity, and also a scattered mind. Before dawn everything is light and subtle: the moment to wake and meditate.

DINACHARYA · A DAY IN HARMONY

A gentle routine for every day

Begin with one or two gestures; your body will ask you for the rest.

  1. Wake early. Before dawn, in the stillness that tradition calls brahma muhurta, the sacred hour, or at least before six.
  2. Let the body eliminate. Without forcing: a body that rises at its hour learns to let go at its hour.
  3. Clean your tongue. With a metal scraper, from back to front: you remove what the body released during the night.
  4. Drink warm water. A glass on an empty stomach awakens the digestive fire.
  5. Massage your body with warm oil. Sesame for vata, coconut for pitta, very little oil or a dry massage for kapha. Then a warm bath.
  6. Move according to your nature. Gently for vata, without competing for pitta, vigorously for kapha. Tradition advises using half your strength, until a little sweat appears on your forehead.
  7. Breathe and meditate. A few minutes of silence set the mind in order for the whole day.
  8. Make lunch your main meal. At midday agni is at its peak. Eat calmly, away from screens.
  9. Have a light, early dinner. A few hours before sleep, so that the night is for rest and not for digestion.
  10. Sleep before ten. While kapha brings calm, sleep comes on its own and the night does its work of renewal.

RITUCHARYA · THE SEASONS

The year breathes too

What the clock is to the day, the seasons are to the year. Ritucharya is the art of living each season according to its nature. Dry cold and wind stir vata; heat kindles pitta; the damp cold of winter builds up kapha, which in spring, when the sun melts it, begins to move. The tradition was born in India, with its six seasons; where the climate is different, the principle is the same.

A dosha builds up quietly during one season and stirs when the next one arrives. That is why the days of transition, which tradition calls ritusandhi, the joining of the seasons, ask us to change our habits little by little, and why they are the natural moments for Panchakarma: what has built up is ready to leave. Tradition teaches that each dosha has its own way out: vamana, the cleansing of the upper tract, for kapha; virechana, gentle intestinal cleansing, for pitta; basti, medicated enemas with oils and herbs, for vata. After cleansing comes the time to nourish: Rasayana.

Here in Florida we live much of the year with heat and humidity, which stir pitta and kapha: it helps to seek coolness without cold and lightness without dryness. And a detail few people notice: air conditioning, cold and dry, can awaken vata indoors.

THE CONSULTATION

How we discover your nature

In the consultation I look for two things: your nature, your prakriti, and what has moved today, your vikriti. It is not an exam: it is a conversation in which your body also speaks.

The pulse. In pulse reading, nadi pariksha, three fingers on your wrist listen to the three doshas: the index finger to vata, the middle finger to pitta, the ring finger to kapha. Tradition describes the way each one moves: vata glides like a snake, pitta jumps like a frog, kapha moves forward like a swan.

The tongue, the eyes, the skin and the voice. Each dosha leaves its signature: vata, dry skin, restless eyes and a quick voice; pitta, warm skin, a penetrating gaze and a clear voice; kapha, soft skin, large serene eyes and a deep voice. The tongue also shows how your digestion is doing and whether there is ama.

Your life. We talk about your digestion, your sleep, your energy and your emotions, because listening is also a way of seeing. With all of this your map takes shape, and I decide what is right for you and when: food, routine, plants, therapies, and whether it is the moment for a Panchakarma or a Rasayana.

QUESTIONS

What many people ask me

Can I find out my dosha with a questionnaire?

It can give you a first idea, but it usually shows your state today, your vikriti, more than your nature: a kapha person under a lot of stress can look like vata. The pulse and the consultation go deeper.

Do I have to change my whole diet?

Not all at once. We begin with a few changes, the ones that help you most. Often, eating warm food, at regular times and calmly, transforms more than changing what you eat.

How do I know if I have ama?

Tradition recognizes it by a coated tongue on waking, heaviness after eating, a poor appetite and a clouded mind. The daily routine helps, and when ama has already settled, Panchakarma is designed to release it from the root.

Does Ayurveda also care for the mind and the soul?

Yes. It describes three qualities of the mind: sattva, clarity; rajas, agitation; tamas, inertia. Everything that balances the doshas, pure food, rhythm, meditation and kindness, makes sattva grow. There Ayurveda and Quantum Transformation meet.
