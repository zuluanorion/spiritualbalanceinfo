Rasayana

The art of rejuvenation

AyurvedaThe three doshasPanchakarmaRasayana

RASA · AYANA

The path of the essence

There is a tiredness that sleep does not take away, and a feeling of having been spent before your time. For that, Ayurveda nourishes, and it nourishes with such care that it calls this an art: Rasayana.

The word joins two Sanskrit roots. Rasa is essence, juice, taste, and it is also the name of the body's first tissue: the plasma, the clear fluid that is born from well-digested food and carries nourishment to every cell. Ayana is path. Rasayana is the path of the essence: the path by which what nourishes you reaches each of your tissues, down to the deepest one, and becomes life.

The classical texts define it as the means of attaining the finest quality of the essence and of all the tissues. It is not about adding something from outside. It is about letting what you eat, breathe, feel and live arrive whole and build you.

It is one of the eight branches of Ayurveda. The classical texts also call it the branch of old age, not because it is only for the elderly, but because it teaches us to travel through the years without the flame of life going out.

THE SEVEN TISSUES

The journey of food within you

What you ate today is making a journey inside you. Ayurveda teaches that the body is made of seven tissues, the dhatus, a word that means “that which sustains.” Each one is formed from the one before it, like a chain of life.

It all begins with digestion. Your digestive fire, agni, turns food into a fine essence that feeds the first tissue; the purest part of each tissue feeds the next, and so on to the seventh. Each tissue also has its own fire, which makes what it receives its own.

Tradition compares this to irrigation water, which fills one field and then flows on to the next: if it arrives clean, every field blossoms; if it stagnates, the last ones dry up. And it teaches that this journey, from the plate to the finest essence, takes about a month. That is why Rasayana is not the work of a single day: it is a sowing.

OJAS · TEJAS · PRANA

The finest essence of all

At the end of the path, something even subtler is distilled from the seven tissues. Ayurveda calls it ojas.

Ojas means vigor, radiance. Just as bees gather honey from many flowers, tradition says, the body gathers ojas from all its tissues. It is described as having the color of ghee, the sweetness of honey and the fragrance of toasted rice, and its seat is the heart. It is the foundation of your natural defenses, the light in your skin and in your eyes, the steadiness of your mind and the quiet joy of being alive. As long as ojas is present, life is sustained.

It has two companions: tejas, the subtle fire of intelligence that shines in the eyes, and prana, the life force that moves with the breath. They are the subtlest form of your three vital energies, the doshas: ojas of kapha (earth and water, what gives form); tejas of pitta (the fire that transforms); prana of vata (air and space, what moves).

Think of an oil lamp. Ojas is the oil, tejas the flame, prana the air that keeps it alive. With oil, the flame burns steady and gives light. Without it, the flame flickers, smokes and begins to burn the wick. That is what exhaustion feels like, and perhaps you know it: the mind stays lit, but it no longer has anything to burn. Rasayana fills the lamp again.

THE SIGNS OF OJAS

How to recognize it

Ojas does not show up in a lab test. It shows up in life: in how you wake up, in how you look at things, in how you get through a difficult day.

WHEN IT IS STRONG

The full lamp

You sleep deeply and wake up with energy. Your skin glows and your eyes are clear. Your body is firm, hunger comes on time and you recover quickly from effort. Your mind is steady, your voice is clear, and there is patience and a joy that needs no reason.

WHEN IT WEAKENS

The flickering flame

Tradition describes it precisely: fear you cannot name, weakness, worry that never stops, tired senses, dull skin, low spirits, a dry body. Many people describe it to me like this: “I sleep but I don't rest,” “everything gets to me.”

WHAT SPENDS IT

What empties the lamp

Too much effort. Sleepless nights. Long fasts, strict diets, dry or hurried meals. Worry, fear, grief and anger. And, gently, the passing of the years. None of this is a fault: it is life, and Rasayana gives back to the body what life asks of it.

AFTER CLEANSING, NOURISHING

Why Rasayana comes after Panchakarma

Over the years, the body stores what it could not digest, a heavy residue that Ayurveda calls ama, which settles in the tissues and in the channels. If we nourish a body that is still burdened, the nourishment does not reach where it should. That is why Ayurveda first cleanses with Panchakarma, its great deep purification, and then nourishes.

The classical texts explain it this way: a dirty cloth does not take the dye; a clean cloth receives it fully. I see it like the earth: a freshly cleansed body receives nourishment the way freshly turned earth receives the seed. When your digestive fire is steady again, Rasayana begins, and what is sown takes root.

Rasayana on its own

It is not always necessary to begin with a cleanse. Ayurveda distinguishes between therapies that lighten and therapies that nourish, and sometimes the body does not need to let go of anything more: it needs to receive. When you have run out of strength, after a long stretch of effort or of caring for others, after grief or after giving birth, and in the second half of life, Rasayana can be the main path.

Over the years, air and space grow in us, the elements of vata: the skin dries, sleep grows light, the mind grows restless. Rasayana brings just the opposite: oil, warmth, sweetness and stability. Even so, I always look first at your digestive fire, so that what nourishes can arrive.

THE DOORS OF RASAYANA

Food and plants

Tradition describes several doors into Rasayana: some you take, others you live.

Food

The first is ahara rasayana, the Rasayana of food. Tradition teaches that the natural sweet taste, the taste of grains, milk, ripe fruit and ghee, is what builds the tissues and ojas most. Especially nourishing are ghee, clarified butter, which kindles the digestive fire without irritating it; milk, when it agrees with you, boiled and warm, with cardamom or ginger, and never with sour fruit or salty food; soaked and peeled almonds, dates, saffron, sesame, grapes and pomegranate; and raw honey, which is never heated: heat makes it heavy and turns it into residue.

How you eat matters too: fresh, warm food made with love, eaten on time, calmly, and only once the previous meal has been digested. What suits each person is different, which is why it is so valuable to know how the three doshas combine in you. And there is no need to change everything at once: it is enough to begin with what you already have in your kitchen.

Plants

The second is aushadha rasayana, the Rasayana of plants (aushadha means medicine). None of them is for everyone: what strengthens one person can weigh another down. That is why they are not recipes; in the consultation I choose which ones suit you, in what form and at what time. These are some of the most cherished:

THE INDIAN GOOSEBERRY

Amalaki

A small, sour fruit that holds five of the six tastes. One of its names means “the nurse,” and tradition considers it the plant that best sustains youth.

THE STRENGTH OF A HORSE

Ashwagandha

Its name means “smell of a horse,” and tradition says it gives the strength of a horse. Warm and grounding, it calms vata, strengthens the tissues, supports deep sleep and restores firmness to those who are exhausted.

THE ROOT THAT NOURISHES

Shatavari

A plant with many tender roots, sweet and cooling. Tradition cherishes it above all for nourishing women through every stage of life, though it nourishes everyone. It calms pitta and vata and feeds the deepest tissues.

THE NECTAR

Guduchi

A climbing vine that tradition calls amrita, the nectar of immortality. It strengthens the body's natural defenses, gently kindles the digestive fire, calms excess heat and balances the three energies.

THE MOTHER WHO CARES

Haritaki

A fruit that tradition loves like a mother: it is said that whoever has no mother at home has haritaki. It calms vata above all, gently clears the channels and brings clarity to the senses.

LONG PEPPER

Pippali

A pungent spice that gently kindles the digestive fire and opens the pathways of breath. Pungent as it is, tradition counts it among the rejuvenating plants: at the end of digestion, it nourishes.

Chyawanprash

The best-known classical preparation is perhaps chyawanprash: a thick, dark paste based on amalaki, cooked with ghee and sesame oil, with dozens of plants and spices, and sweetened with honey once it has cooled. Its name recalls an old sage who, according to the legends of India, regained his youth with it.

THE MIND · THE WAY WE LIVE

Rasayana for the mind and for life

The third door is medhya rasayana, the Rasayana of the mind. Medha is intelligence, and tradition teaches that it has three movements: understanding, holding what has been understood, and remembering it. Some plants are especially cherished for nourishing them:

  • Brahmi, whose name recalls Brahman, infinite consciousness: it cools, clarifies and quiets the mind.
  • Shankhapushpi, “the conch flower,” which soothes and supports good sleep.
  • Mandukaparni, with small round leaves, cherished for memory and calm.
  • Yashtimadhu, “the honey stick,” the sweet root of licorice, which soothes and nourishes the voice and the mind.

The way we live

The fourth door is the most beautiful, and it costs nothing: achara rasayana, the Rasayana of conduct. The classical texts teach that whoever lives this way receives its fruits even without taking a single plant. Read it without hurry; it is not a list of duties, but the portrait of a life that nourishes:

  • Speaking the truth
  • Living free of anger
  • Compassion, doing no harm
  • Moderation in food, work and pleasure
  • Cleanliness, outside and in
  • Devotion and prayer
  • Respect for elders, teachers and the sacred
  • Generosity
  • Gentle, peaceful speech
  • Rest, meditation and an inner life

Here Ayurveda and Quantum Transformation, the heart of my work, meet. Perhaps you have already felt it in yourself: anger burns ojas; fear and worry scatter it like the wind. Truth, peace and love fill it again. That is why devotion and prayer are not an ornament on this list: they are nourishment, of the finest kind. When the soul is set in order, the body receives.

AJASRIKA

Everyday Rasayana

The fifth door is ajasrika rasayana, the Rasayana of every day. The classical texts give milk and ghee in the daily diet as an example; I extend it to the small habits of dinacharya, Ayurveda's daily routine, which, repeated with love, nourish more than any isolated effort:

  1. Wake early. At sunrise or a little before, when the mind is clearest.
  2. Kindle your fire. Gently scrape your tongue and drink warm water.
  3. Warm oil on the skin. Before your bath, a few minutes of abhyanga: your own hands bringing warm oil to your whole body. Tradition says that oil every day keeps away old age, fatigue and excess vata.
  4. Eat on time. Your main meal at midday, when the digestive fire is strongest, and an early, light dinner.
  5. A moment of silence. A few minutes of meditation or conscious breathing at the start and the end of the day.
  6. Prepare for rest. Warm oil on the soles of your feet, soft light, no screens, and early to bed.

None of these gestures seems like much, but Rasayana is more like a gentle rain than a storm: what falls every day is what truly soaks the earth.

IN MY CLINIC

How Rasayana is lived in the clinic

It all begins in the consultation. I look at your prakriti, the nature you were born with, and your vikriti, what is out of balance today. I listen to your digestive fire, your tissues, your ojas and the moment of your life, and from there I trace your path.

It often begins with oil. The oil therapies, snehana, are a kind of care the body understands without words. Abhyanga, with warm medicated oils chosen for you, nourishes and calms, and tradition teaches that, with constancy, the oil reaches tissue by tissue into the deepest layers. Shirodhara, a continuous stream of warm oil over the forehead, nourishes the mind and brings a very deep calm.

To this are added the foods and plants chosen for you, a routine for each day and for each season (ritucharya), rest and meditation.

Tradition describes two ways of living it: in retreat, sheltered from the world (kutipraveshika, “entering the hut”), or in the midst of daily life (vatatapika, “in the wind and sun”). Today it is almost always lived the second way. I decide when to begin and how long it lasts according to your nature, your age, the season and what you are going through: sometimes a few weeks, sometimes a whole season, and sometimes it stays with you as a way of living.

RASAYANA AND THE SPIRIT

Returning to the essence

Rasayana is the path of the essence. And the essence, in the end, does not belong only to the body.

The tradition of India says of the Eternal: “He is rasa,” the essence itself, the taste of bliss. When food reaches your finest tissue and becomes ojas, the body does in its own way what the soul does in meditation: it returns to its essence.

Ayurveda teaches that ojas lives in the heart, the same place where it locates consciousness, and it advises caring for it by turning away from what afflicts the heart, seeking what gladdens it, and cultivating serenity and knowledge. Devotion, prayer, gratitude and love are not foreign to medicine: they are its subtlest form.

Growing older with grace

If at times you feel older than you would like, read this calmly: Ayurveda sees aging as part of nature, like hunger or sleep. Rasayana does not promise to stop time: it helps the years ripen you instead of wearing you down. The last stage of life belongs to vata, to the subtlest elements, air and space. If ojas sustains it, that subtlety stops being fragility and becomes clarity, silence and wisdom. The body grows lighter, and the Self shows itself more clearly.

QUESTIONS

What many people ask me

Do I have to do Panchakarma before Rasayana?

Not always. For a deep Rasayana, cleansing first is ideal, because a clean body receives better. But sometimes what is needed is nourishment. In the consultation I see what your case calls for.

Is Rasayana only for older people?

No. Tradition recommends it above all in youth and in the middle of life, when the body responds most strongly, to build firm ojas. In the later years it accompanies you with a special sweetness.

Can I take Rasayana plants on my own?

They are noble plants, but they are not the same for everyone: if the digestive fire is weak, a very nourishing preparation can become heavy. That is why I choose them in the consultation. The Rasayana of conduct and everyday Rasayana, on the other hand, you can begin today.

How long does it last, and when do the changes show?

Many people feel the first changes soon: deeper sleep, more calm, more desire to live. The deepest part takes time, because food takes about a month to reach the finest tissue. That is why we speak of weeks and months, and everyday Rasayana is for life.
